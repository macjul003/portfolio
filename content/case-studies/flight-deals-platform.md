## The problem

The platform had great deals — genuinely good prices on flights across dozens of routes. But the homepage was a firehose: hundreds of deals, minimal filtering, no sense of what was relevant to any individual user.

Users were bouncing before they found anything useful. The deals were good; the discovery was broken.

## Research

I ran a card-sort study with 24 users and five days of session recordings. Two things stood out:

- Users didn't think in terms of destinations — they thought in terms of *budgets* and *vibes* (“I want somewhere warm for under ₹30k”)
- The most engaged users were those who had set a home airport — every other deal felt irrelevant to everyone else

The platform was showing a catalogue when users needed a recommendation.

## Design decisions

### Onboarding around intent, not preferences

I replaced the blank homepage with a 3-question intent capture on first visit: home airport, rough budget, and travel style (beach, city, adventure). No account required. Answers were stored locally and used immediately to rank and filter the deal feed.

### Deal cards redesigned around savings clarity

The old card showed price, destination, and airline. I restructured it around the saving: “₹12,400 saved vs. typical price” in large type, with the destination and dates secondary. This reframing shifted the emotional hook from “is this a good price?” (requiring research) to “this is clearly a deal” (immediate).

### Flexible departure radius

A map filter let users set a departure radius — pulling in deals from airports within driving distance. For users in metro areas with multiple airports, this meaningfully expanded the deal pool without requiring them to search each airport manually.

## Outcome

- **CTR on deal cards** increased 58% in the first month post-launch
- **Bounce rate** dropped from 71% to 44%
- **Return visits within 7 days** increased 2.1×
