<p>For the past three years, I've written something every day.<br>A few lines, a thought, sometimes just a single word. At some point, I began turning those entries into images using Midjourney.</p>
<p>It wasn't about making beautiful visuals.<br>It was about seeing what my thoughts looked like when I gave them form.</p>
<p>Some images came out strange, others unexpectedly moving. Most didn't match what I had in mind, but that mismatch became the most interesting part. It showed how vague ideas shift once they meet structure.</p>
<p>Over time, this routine stopped being about output. It became a quiet practice of reflection.<br>Writing captured the thought. The image caught the emotion that words couldn't.</p>
<p>Looking back, I think creativity isn't about control or perfection.<br>It's about noticing, the change in tone, the rhythm of thought, the space between what you meant and what appeared.</p>
<p>The more I notice, the more I understand how design begins.<br>In attention, not intention.</p>
