<p>Learning isn't just about reading words. It's about making ideas come alive.<br>I've been experimenting with a way to do that — by using Midjourney to create visual representations of the concepts I study.</p>
<p>Lately, I've been diving into user adoption in product development. It's a dense topic with theories like <em>Loss Aversion</em> and <em>Critical Mass</em>. Instead of just reading about them, I started turning each concept into an image.</p>
<p>The result surprised me. Weeks later, I could still recall what I learned, almost as if the images had carved the ideas deeper into memory.</p>
<p>I'm beginning to see learning itself as a design process. Reading gives you the structure. Visualization gives it form.<br>When you translate an idea into an image, you're forced to understand it well enough to recreate it.</p>
<p>This isn't about aesthetics. It's about comprehension — turning abstraction into something you can see, not just think about.</p>
<p>I plan to keep exploring this approach, using Midjourney as a creative partner in learning. It's a reminder that sometimes, the best way to remember is to redesign how you learn.</p>
